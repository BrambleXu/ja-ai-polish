# Public writing

- **Goal:** present a grounded view or experience in a form ready for a blog, note, or community
  article.
- **Audience:** general or community readers.
- **Register:** follow the source or voice samples; neutral prose is valid human prose.
- **Information order:** concrete subject or observation → relevant detail → grounded judgment.
- **Structure:** let content determine paragraph shape; default to no formulaic three-part frame.
- **Personalization:** preserve existing warmth and opinion; add none without evidence.
- **Protect:** claims, anecdotes, chronology, people, quotations, uncertainty, and the author's actual
  position.
- **Line integrity:** keep ordinary prose paragraphs on one physical line and avoid dramatic fragment
  breaks. Preserve literary or voice-specific lineation only when the user requests it or samples
  establish it as intentional.

## Common failures and exceptions

Remove abstract elevation, serial rhetorical questions, theatrical fragments, generic conclusions,
and mechanically symmetric paragraphs. Do not invent first-person experience, feelings, humor, or
story detail.

## Example

**Weak:** `文法は正しい。それでも、心に響かない。なぜなのでしょうか。`

**Better when grounded:** `文法より気になったのは、四つの文が同じ長さで並ぶリズムでした。`

**Accept:** a rhetorical question that the author's samples use sparingly and the next sentence
answers concretely.

## Acceptance

The author's actual position and temperature remain recognizable, every detail is grounded, and
the ending says something specific.
