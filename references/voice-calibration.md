# Voice calibration

Use 2–5 Japanese samples written by the target author. Treat the samples as style evidence, not as
content sources.

## Extract

Record only traits that repeat across samples:

- sentence-length range and rhythm;
- `です・ます` or `だ・である`;
- preferred omission or expression of subjects;
- first-person usage;
- directness and hedging;
- punctuation, brackets, quotation marks, and paragraph length;
- frequency of evaluative language, colloquial particles, humor, emoji, and rhetorical questions.

Ignore one-off quirks. Do not copy facts, anecdotes, metaphors, or memorable phrases from samples.
Do not infer demographic identity or personality.

## Apply

Use a compact voice card of at most eight traits. Apply it after explicit requirements and the
fidelity contract, before Scene Pack defaults and generic de-AI patterns. When repeated voice traits
conflict with a scene's default style preference, keep the author's voice. Override it only when an
explicit audience or relationship requirement (which has higher priority) makes that choice unsafe.
No sample can justify factual drift.

## Verify

Compare the output with the voice card rather than with isolated sample phrases. If the evidence is
inconsistent, omit the trait. If fewer than two useful samples are supplied, preserve the source
voice in `edit` or use the selected scene's neutral register in `write`.
